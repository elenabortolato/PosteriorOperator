A conjugate model: Dirichlet-Multinomial, exact posterior in closed form
========================================================================================
  Theta ~ Dirichlet(alpha), Y ~ Multinomial(N, Theta)
  posterior = Dirichlet(alpha + y), exact -- no grid, no MCMC, no ABC
  40000 simulations shared by both methods, rank 64, 50 observations scored

  W1 is to the EXACT marginal, in units of the prior sd. Coverage is of
  nominal 90% intervals against the theta that generated the data.

Reading
========================================================================================
  The answer is chi^2, not dimension, and the two tables say so separately.

  DIMENSION (table 1), with chi^2 held near 5: NCP wins at 2 dimensions,
  loses at 4, and wins again at 9 and 19 -- no monotone decay. Its error
  PLATEAUS from 9 to 19 dimensions while NPE's accelerates, so at 19 it is
  ahead by a factor of 1.8 with exactly nominal coverage. The prediction
  that an atom-reweighting estimator must collapse geometrically in the
  dimension is simply wrong at fixed chi^2, and the reason is in the
  post/prior column: holding chi^2 fixed while adding parameters pushes the
  posterior back towards the prior, which is exactly where prior atoms are
  dense. Dimension and atom coverage are not independent; chi^2 sets both.
  The mechanism favouring NCP is that it never estimates a density over
  theta at all -- a marginal is a weighted average, one-dimensional however
  large p is, while NPE must fit a 19-dimensional joint.

  CONCENTRATION (table 2), at fixed dimension: the NCP/NPE ratio rises
  monotonically with chi^2 -- 0.61, 0.91, 1.15, 1.51, 3.51, 14.5 across four
  orders of magnitude, with no reversal. NCP's absolute error climbs
  (0.027 -> 0.230) while NPE's is roughly flat (0.044 -> 0.016), so this is
  NCP degrading as information arrives, not NPE improving. That is the
  truncation: chi^2 is the mass a rank-d SVD has to capture, and at
  chi^2 = 3700 rank 64 cannot.

  THE CONTROL. At low chi^2 the posterior is close to the prior, so a lazy
  estimator could score well by doing nothing. The 'prior W1' column is the
  score for doing exactly that. Both methods beat it everywhere, and at 19
  dimensions NCP captures about 72% of the available improvement over the
  prior against NPE's 48% -- so the high-dimensional win is real, not an
  artifact of staying put.

  WHEN TO USE WHICH. Prefer the operator when chi^2 is small, and the chi^2
  you can afford grows with dimension: the crossover is near chi^2 = 4 at 4
  dimensions but NCP still wins at chi^2 = 5.3 at 19. Both quantities are
  estimable from the fit itself -- chi^2 as sum(sigma_k^2), or sigma_1 alone,
  which tracks the crossover just as well (0.42, 0.59, 0.78, 0.92, 0.98) and
  is free. This is a decision rule you can check before trusting an answer,
  which is worth more than an unconditional accuracy claim.
