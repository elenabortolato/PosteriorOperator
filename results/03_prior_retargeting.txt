  The first row is the identity check: reweighting by a constant leaves the
  posterior untouched, and its error is the low-rank fit's own error.

  The rows below it carry a caveat worth stating in the write-up. The
  identity E_pi[f|y] = E_q[f w|y] / E_q[w|y] is exact in population, but the
  weights multiply the ESTIMATED proposal posterior, so the estimator's error
  does not cancel -- it interacts with the weights. Because the low-rank
  posterior is over-dispersed (see 04_identifiability.py), the product leans
  on the new prior more than the truth does, and retargeting to a
  concentrated prior AMPLIFIES the error rather than leaving it unchanged.
  So the honest claim is: retargeting is free of retraining, not free of
  error, and its accuracy degrades with how far pi sits from q.

  The effective sample size is the companion diagnostic. The hard
  constraint is the extreme case: it deletes the draws outside its support,
  so the ESS falls to roughly the prior mass of the constraint.

======================================================================================
no-retraining check: is a retargeted fit as good as one trained under the prior?
======================================================================================
  target prior: Gaussian at (0.6, 0.2), sd 0.5
  retargeted from the flat fit (20000 draws reweighted): mean |error| 0.3127
  refitted on 11687 draws from that prior:          mean |error| 0.1072

  The refit is 2.9x more accurate, and it lands at roughly the
  flat fit's own error -- so the gap is the price of retargeting, not of the
  smaller simulation budget. Retargeting buys the ability to answer a prior
  chosen after training at no extra simulation cost; it does not match a
  refit. Worth stating that way rather than as equivalence, and it suggests
  the more ambitious route in the paper's Section 9 -- folding the weights
  into the training objective itself, so the operator is fitted under the
  target prior rather than corrected afterwards -- is the one that would
  actually close this gap.
