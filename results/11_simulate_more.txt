============================================================================================
Reading
============================================================================================
  The two configurations answer the question in OPPOSITE directions, and
  that is the result.

  At chi^2 = 5, more simulations work -- but only at a rank where truncation
  is already spent. Sixteen times the budget buys 8% at rank 8 and 68% at
  rank 128. At low rank you are truncation-limited and data cannot help; at
  high rank every remaining nat is estimation error, which data fixes. So
  'simulate more' pays in proportion to how much of the error is NOT
  truncation, and the reported tail is what tells you that proportion.

  At chi^2 = 102, the same sixteen-fold increase buys 3%, and the captured
  fraction actually FALLS (37.2% to 34.3%). Neither rank nor sample size is
  binding there: the networks cannot represent the remaining directions, so
  the fitted spectrum runs out of mass to place. Capacity is the untested
  third knob.

  THE SELF-CHECK PROPOSED ABOVE DOES NOT WORK. sigma_hat_D / sigma_hat_1 is
  0.003, 0.004, 0.000 down the chi^2 = 102 block -- the spectrum has
  apparently died well inside the fitted rank, which was meant to certify
  that D was generous -- while the fit captures barely a third of the true
  chi^2. The check fails precisely because a capacity-limited fit looks
  converged from the inside: its singular values collapse because the
  networks have run out of representable directions, not because the true
  spectrum has decayed. A decayed sigma_hat is therefore NOT evidence that
  the rank was sufficient, and this diagnostic should not be used.

  What survives is the coarser one. sigma_1 is 0.92 here against 0.67 in the
  benign configuration, and chi^2_hat is about 35 even understated threefold
  -- both large, both observable, and example 09 already identifies that as
  the regime where the method loses. The regime indicator works; the
  fine-grained sufficiency check does not.
