Reading
========================================================================================
  The left panel of the first figure is the claim. If the points sit on the
  diagonal, the fitted spectrum is an honest report of the fit's own error,
  and a user can read it off without owning a reference posterior. Points
  BELOW the diagonal mean the fit is flattering itself -- the tail it can
  see stops at its own rank, so whatever mass the truncation never captured
  is invisible to it. The 'captured' percentage above says how much of the
  true chi^2 the fit accounts for, and that is the honest ceiling on how
  much of its error it can possibly report.

  The right panel is what makes it useful rather than merely true: the same
  reported number ordering the actual error in the posterior marginals.

  The second figure answers what the error numbers mean. A W_1 of 0.07 in
  units of the prior standard deviation is hard to interpret in the
  abstract; the marginals show it directly, and show NPE's curves drifting
  off the exact posterior as the dimension grows while the operator's stay
  on it.
