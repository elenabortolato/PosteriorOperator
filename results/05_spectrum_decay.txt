Reading
==============================================================================
  The GEOMETRIC law fits better than the polynomial one on every benchmark,
  and on the exact Gaussian spectrum too -- R^2 around 0.98-0.99 against
  0.83-0.90. So the polynomial-decay assumption behind the rank-selection
  bound is not what these models exhibit; they decay faster than that.

  That is good news for the bound rather than bad. Geometric decay makes
  sum_{k>d} sigma_k^2 fall geometrically in d, so the truncation-bias term is
  far smaller than a Sobolev-type polynomial assumption would allow, and the
  oracle rank grows only logarithmically in the target accuracy instead of
  polynomially. Worth stating the bound under geometric decay as the primary
  case, with polynomial decay as the conservative fallback.

  Two caveats on the numbers themselves. The fitted rates are OPTIMISTIC, by
  the calibration above: the estimated tail undershoots the truth at this n,
  so both the exponents and the truncation tails flatter the method. And the
  models differ enormously in how hard they are -- AR(2) needs d = 8 to
  capture 99% of sum sigma_k^2, while g-and-k still leaves 23% outside d = 16.
  Rank selection is genuinely model-specific, not a constant to be fixed once.
