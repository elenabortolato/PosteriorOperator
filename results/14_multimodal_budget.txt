Reading
================================================================================================
  Rank is the lever, budget is not: at fixed width, rank 16 to 256 moves
  W1 from 0.3262 to 0.2011 while four times the simulations moves it from
  0.3262 to 0.3290. The defect is approximation, not estimation.

  Raising rank AND width together cures the specific defect completely.
  Valley mass falls 0.0815, 0.1075, 0.0465, 0.0386, 0.0208, 0.0021 across
  ranks 32 to 1024 -- a fortyfold reduction, and essentially zero at the
  top. The smoothing account of example 13 was right, and spectral terms
  are its cure.

  BUT THE AGGREGATE ERROR PLATEAUS ANYWAY. W1 falls 0.3413, 0.2885, 0.2049,
  0.1742, 0.1357, 0.1312: a steady factor of about 0.80 per doubling that
  stops at the last step, where it is 0.97. It is levelling off near 0.13,
  more than three times NPE's 0.0386, so more rank will NOT close the gap
  on this model. An extrapolation of the 0.80 rate -- which this script
  originally reported -- predicted that rank 27000 would match NPE. That
  was wrong, because the rate stops holding exactly where it mattered.

  The figure shows why. The error changes character rather than shrinking.
  At low rank the estimate over-smooths and fills the valley; at rank 1024
  both modes are resolved and the valley is empty, but the curve is jagged,
  with high-frequency wiggles and an over-tall mode -- ringing from the
  truncated expansion plus noise in the newly-added directions. One error
  source is cured and another grows to take its place.

  chi^2_hat doubles from 9.3 to 113 and only then slows, reaching 173.7 at
  rank 1024. It never saturates, so this ratio is simply not low-rank, and
  the diagnostic says so from the fit alone.

  Cost makes the verdict unambiguous. Rank 1024 takes 1914s against NPE's
  132s -- fourteen times the training time for three and a half times the
  error. Sharp multimodality is not an intrinsic limitation of the
  operator, but on this model it is not worth buying out of.
